### 2. Extend the typed request into an executable plan

Represent a bounded ordered plan containing:

- Plan/version and original question.
- Requested outcomes and individual requirements.
- Step IDs, operations and dependencies.
- Approved measures, dimensions, filters and exclusions.
- Periods, grain, baseline and partial-period policy.
- Ranking basis, direction, limits and tie handling.

- Typed bindings to earlier step results.
- Assumptions, ambiguities and unsupported requirements.

An evidence list containing `depends_on` is not sufficient. Dependencies must control execution.

Initially support reusable operations such as aggregate, select extreme, filter by previous result, compare, rank and retrieve history. Reject unsupported combinations explicitly.

Start with a configurable maximum of **four analytical steps**.

### 3. Bind dependent results correctly

A later step must consume the authorized output of its declared predecessor.

Examples:

- The selected month becomes the later date filter.
- The selected region becomes the later geographic filter.
- Ranked item IDs become the fixed cohort for historical analysis.

Use canonical identities and typed values. Never concatenate returned strings into executable SQL.

Prefer CTEs when the complete operation can execute safely in one statement. Otherwise, use bounded sequential queries with a coherent data snapshot.

Do not change transaction isolation after queries have already started.

Define behavior for ties, multiple selected values, missing results and oversized cohorts. Never arbitrarily choose one tied result or broaden the scope.

### 4. Make business meaning explicit

Use approved semantic policies for comparison defaults, ranking and incomplete periods.

Distinguish:

- Lowest value from largest decline.
- Absolute change from percentage change.
- Contribution to a decline from a positive offset.
- Separate region/model breakdowns from combined region–model groups.

Clarify material ambiguity when no approved default applies.

For extreme-period selection, apply the approved completeness rule. Excluding a partial period and flagging a partial period are different policies.

Do not infer completeness from `MAX(date)` or observed date coverage alone.

- Scope-aware caching and semantic-version checks.

Recheck permissions before dependent execution and before returning results.

Do not add an LLM call per step. Do not retry permission denials.

Bound analytical queries, total time, selected values and corrective attempts. If the plan exceeds its budget, clarify or offer a smaller scope rather than truncating it silently.

### 6. Add independent coverage and result validation

- Required outputs are representable.
- Measures, dimensions, periods and scope are approved.
- The plan retains comparisons, exclusions and requested groupings.

After compilation, verify that SQL implements the plan, including dependency filters, ranking basis and required columns.

After execution, verify:

- Correct selected period, region or cohort.
- Current and baseline coverage.
- Missing observations versus verified zero.

- Caps, ties and partial results.

Do not claim reconciliation merely because “Other” was calculated as the difference. Validate the underlying decomposition and use an independently obtained headline where appropriate.

A failed check must block a successful-looking answer. Use a bounded correction, focused clarification or clearly labelled partial result.
