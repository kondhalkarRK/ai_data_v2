# Automobile Business Process Handbook

**Owner:** Sales Operations, Dealer Development and Motor Insurance Operations
**Applies to:** All authorised Dealers, Regional Sales Offices and the Motor Insurance desk in India
**Version:** 2026.3
**Related document:** Automobile Business Glossary (canonical definitions of Vehicle, Dealer, Customer, Region, Revenue, Sales, Policy and Claim)

This handbook describes how a Vehicle moves from a Dealer's showroom to a Customer's driveway, how Dealers are run and measured, and how the motor insurance Policy and Claim processes work for Vehicles we sell. Each process lists the steps, the people involved, the systems of record, the business rules, and the KPIs used to measure it.

---

## 1. Vehicle Sales Process

### 1.1 Purpose

The Vehicle Sales Process turns a Customer enquiry into an invoiced Sale and a delivered Vehicle. It is the source of every Revenue, Units Sold, Orders and Average Selling Price figure in management reporting.

### 1.2 Participants

- **Customer** — the buyer of the Vehicle.
- **Salesperson** — the Dealer employee who owns the enquiry through to delivery.
- **Dealer** — the authorised outlet that raises the invoice.
- **Regional Sales Office** — allocates Vehicle stock and monitors targets for the Region.
- **Finance Partner** — the bank or NBFC that funds a vehicle loan, when used.

### 1.3 Steps

1. **Enquiry.** A Customer contacts a Dealer by walk-in, phone, website or marketplace lead. The Dealer records the enquiry and assigns a Salesperson.
2. **Test drive and configuration.** The Customer chooses a Vehicle (make, model, body type such as SUV or Sedan, engine type) and a Colour Variant.
3. **Quotation.** The Salesperson issues a quotation showing ex-showroom price, registration, insurance premium, accessories and any offers.
4. **Booking.** The Customer pays a booking amount. A booking is not yet a Sale and does not count towards Revenue.
5. **Allocation.** The Regional Sales Office allocates a Vehicle from stock to the booking. Popular SUVs may have a waiting period of several weeks.
6. **Finance and insurance.** If a loan is needed, the Finance Partner approves it. A motor insurance Policy is issued on the Vehicle before registration (see section 4).
7. **Invoicing — the Sale.** The Dealer raises the invoice. At this point a Sale is recorded with the Vehicle, colour, Salesperson, Dealer, Region, sales date, quantity, price per unit and total sale value.
8. **Registration and delivery.** The Vehicle is registered with the transport authority and handed to the Customer. The Customer now owns the Vehicle.

### 1.4 Business rules

- A Sale is recognised on the invoice date (sales date). Delivery date is tracked separately.
- A Sale is recorded against the Region of the selling Dealer.
- Cancelled invoices are reversed and excluded from Revenue in the month of cancellation.
- Fleet Sales (five or more Vehicles to one company in a year) are flagged so they can be excluded from retail comparisons.

### 1.5 KPIs

| KPI | How it is calculated | Typical review |
|-----|----------------------|----------------|
| Revenue | Sum of total sale value of all Sales | Monthly regional review |
| Units Sold | Sum of Vehicle quantity across Sales | Weekly sales call |
| Orders | Count of distinct Sales | Weekly sales call |
| Average Selling Price | Revenue ÷ Units Sold | Monthly pricing review |
| Target Achievement | Units Sold ÷ Target Units | Monthly regional review |
| SUV Share | SUV Units Sold ÷ Units Sold | Quarterly product review |

### 1.6 Example questions

- "Show total sales per year."
- "Revenue by Region and month for 2025 compared with target revenue."
- "Which Salesperson sold the most SUVs last quarter?"
- "Average selling price of Sedans by Dealer Grade."

## 2. Customer Purchase Journey

### 2.1 Purpose

The Customer Purchase Journey describes the experience from the Customer's point of view. It explains where Customers drop out and which touchpoints lead to a Sale.

### 2.2 Journey stages

| Stage | What the Customer does | What we record | Conversion measure |
|-------|------------------------|----------------|--------------------|
| Awareness | Sees advertising or a recommendation | Campaign source | Leads per campaign |
| Consideration | Compares Vehicles online and visits a Dealer | Enquiry, preferred Vehicle | Enquiry-to-test-drive rate |
| Test drive | Drives the Vehicle at the Dealer or at home | Test drive record | Test-drive-to-booking rate |
| Booking | Pays a booking amount | Booking, chosen Colour Variant | Booking-to-invoice rate |
| Purchase | Receives the invoice and Policy | Sale, Policy | — |
| Delivery | Takes delivery of the Vehicle | Delivery date | Delivery within promised date |
| Ownership | Services the Vehicle, renews the Policy, may raise a Claim | Service visits, renewals, Claims | Renewal Rate, repeat purchase |

### 2.3 Key relationships in the journey

- A Customer enquires at a Dealer.
- A Customer test-drives a Vehicle.
- A Customer buys a Vehicle from a Dealer, which creates a Sale.
- A Customer owns the Vehicle after delivery.
- A Customer holds a Policy that covers the Vehicle.
- A Customer may raise a Claim linked to that Policy.

### 2.4 Business rules

- A **First-time Buyer** has no earlier Vehicle registered with any of our Dealers.
- A **Repeat Buyer** has bought at least one earlier Vehicle; repeat purchase within five years is the main loyalty measure.
- A Customer who books but does not invoice within 90 days is marked as a lost booking.

### 2.5 Example questions

- "How many first-time buyers bought an SUV in the South region in 2025?"
- "What share of Customers buy a Policy at the Dealer when they buy the Vehicle?"
- "Repeat purchase rate by Region."

## 3. Dealer Operations

### 3.1 Purpose

Dealer Operations covers how the authorised Dealer network is set up, staffed, supplied, measured and graded. Dealer performance drives most of the regional numbers.

### 3.2 Dealer structure

- Every Dealer has a unique dealer code, a name, a city and exactly one Region.
- A Dealer employs Salespeople; each Salesperson belongs to one Dealer at a time.
- A Dealer may run one main showroom and several satellite outlets, all invoiced under one dealer code.
- Each Region (North, South, East, West) is managed by a Regional Sales Manager who owns the Region's targets.

### 3.3 Monthly operating cycle

1. **Target setting.** At the start of each month the Regional Sales Office sets Target Units and Target Revenue per make of Vehicle, and splits them across Dealers in the Region.
2. **Stock allocation.** Vehicles are allocated to Dealers based on bookings, target and past sell-through.
3. **Selling.** Dealers run the Vehicle Sales Process (section 1).
4. **Mid-month review.** Regional managers review Units Sold against target and move stock between Dealers where needed.
5. **Month-end close.** Sales are frozen, Revenue is reported, and target achievement is calculated per Dealer and per Region.
6. **Incentive calculation.** Dealer and Salesperson incentives are paid on target achievement and Customer satisfaction.

### 3.4 Dealer Grade review

Dealer Grades are reviewed every quarter:

- **Grade A** — target achievement of 100% or more for three consecutive months and a Customer satisfaction score of at least 85.
- **Grade B** — target achievement between 80% and 99%.
- **Grade C** — target achievement below 80%, or the Dealer opened within the last 12 months.

A Dealer that stays in Grade C for three quarters enters a Dealer Development plan with the Regional Sales Office.

### 3.5 KPIs

| KPI | Meaning |
|-----|---------|
| Dealer Revenue | Revenue from Sales invoiced by the Dealer |
| Dealer Units Sold | Units Sold by the Dealer |
| Target Achievement | Dealer Units Sold ÷ Dealer Target Units |
| Active Salespeople | Number of Salespeople with at least one Sale in the month |
| Revenue per Salesperson | Dealer Revenue ÷ Active Salespeople |
| Insurance Attach Rate | Policies issued at the Dealer ÷ Vehicles sold by the Dealer |

### 3.6 Example questions

- "Top 10 Dealers by revenue in the North region this quarter."
- "Which Grade C Dealers improved target achievement the most this year?"
- "Revenue per Salesperson by Dealer Grade."

## 4. Insurance Policy Flow

### 4.1 Purpose

The Insurance Policy Flow describes how a motor insurance Policy is quoted, issued, earned and renewed for a Vehicle. Most new-vehicle Policies are sold at the Dealer during the Vehicle Sales Process.

### 4.2 Participants

- **Customer (Policyholder)** — holds the Policy.
- **Dealer** — acts as the point of sale for Policies on new Vehicles.
- **Underwriting desk** — approves non-standard risks and pricing.
- **Motor Insurance Operations** — issues, endorses, cancels and renews Policies.

### 4.3 Steps

1. **Quote.** The Dealer requests a quote for the Vehicle using make, model, engine type, city, and the Customer's details. Three products are offered: Comprehensive, Third-party only, and Own-damage only.
2. **Underwriting.** Standard risks are priced automatically. High-value SUVs, commercial use and customers with poor claims history go to the underwriting desk.
3. **Issue.** On payment of premium, the Policy is issued. The Policy covers exactly one Vehicle and is held by one Customer.
4. **In force.** The Policy is in force from its start date to its end date, normally one year. Premium is earned evenly month by month.
5. **Endorsement.** Changes such as address, financier or accessory cover are made through endorsements.
6. **Renewal.** Thirty days before the end date the Customer is invited to renew. A renewed Policy continues cover for the same Vehicle.
7. **Lapse or cancellation.** A Policy not renewed within the grace period lapses. Cancelled Policies stop earning premium from the cancellation date.

### 4.4 Key relationships

- A Policy covers a Vehicle.
- A Policy is held by a Customer.
- A Policy is sold by a Dealer (for new Vehicles).
- Premium is earned from a Policy.
- A Claim is linked to a Policy (see section 5).

### 4.5 KPIs

| KPI | How it is calculated |
|-----|----------------------|
| Written Premium | Total premium on Policies issued in the period |
| Earned Premium | Premium relating to the months the Policy was in force in the period |
| Renewal Rate | Policies renewed ÷ Policies due for renewal |
| Insurance Attach Rate | Policies sold at Dealers ÷ Vehicles sold |
| Policies in Force | Count of Policies active on the last day of the period |

### 4.6 Example questions

- "Renewal rate by Region for Policies sold at the Dealer."
- "Earned premium by product and month."
- "Insurance attach rate for SUVs compared with Sedans."

## 5. Claims Lifecycle

### 5.1 Purpose

The Claims Lifecycle describes how a Claim is reported, assessed, decided and paid. Claims drive Loss Ratio, the most important profitability KPI for motor insurance.

### 5.2 Participants

- **Customer (Policyholder)** — reports the Claim.
- **Claims Handler** — owns the Claim from registration to closure.
- **Surveyor** — inspects the damaged Vehicle and estimates repair cost.
- **Workshop** — repairs the Vehicle; often the Dealer's own service workshop.

### 5.3 Stages

1. **Reported.** The Customer reports the incident. A Claim is registered and linked to the Policy that covers the Vehicle. The reported date is recorded.
2. **Under survey.** A Surveyor inspects the Vehicle and estimates the loss. An initial reserve (incurred amount) is set.
3. **Approved or rejected.** The Claims Handler checks the Policy was in force on the loss date and the cause is covered. The Claim is approved or rejected.
4. **Repair and settlement.** For approved Claims the Vehicle is repaired at a Workshop, or a total-loss settlement is agreed. Payment is made to the Workshop or the Customer.
5. **Closed.** The Claim is closed once all payments and recoveries are complete. The final incurred amount is fixed.

### 5.4 Business rules

- A Claim is always linked to exactly one Policy, and through it to one Vehicle and one Customer.
- A Claim on a Policy that was not in force on the loss date is rejected.
- Incurred amount = amount paid + outstanding reserve.
- Claims reported more than 30 days after the loss date are flagged for review.

### 5.5 KPIs

| KPI | How it is calculated |
|-----|----------------------|
| Loss Ratio | Incurred claim amount ÷ Earned Premium |
| Claim Frequency | Number of Claims ÷ exposure (vehicle-years insured) |
| Average Claim Severity | Incurred claim amount ÷ number of Claims |
| Approval Rate | Approved Claims ÷ decided Claims |
| Average Settlement Time | Average days from reported date to settlement |

### 5.6 Example questions

- "Loss ratio by Vehicle segment for Policies written in 2025."
- "Claim frequency for SUVs versus Sedans by Region."
- "Average claim severity by month for Claims linked to Dealer-sold Policies."

## 6. How the processes connect

The five processes form one chain from the showroom to the claim:

- A **Customer** enquires at a **Dealer** located in a **Region**.
- The Customer buys a **Vehicle** from the Dealer, creating a **Sale** that contributes to **Revenue** and **Units Sold**.
- A **Policy** is issued that **covers** the Vehicle and is held by the Customer.
- If the Vehicle is damaged, the Customer raises a **Claim** that is **linked to** the Policy.
- **Loss Ratio** is calculated from Claims and earned Premium, and can be analysed by Vehicle, Dealer and Region because every Claim traces back through the Policy to the Vehicle and the Dealer that sold it.

This chain is why a single question such as "Which Regions have the highest claim frequency on Vehicles sold by their own Dealers?" can be answered end to end.
